Who We Are

About Florida Kimono

A statewide community for everyone who loves kimono — collectors, sellers, educators, artists, and the simply curious.

Why We Exist

Purpose & Mission

Our purpose

To cultivate a vibrant, supportive community where the traditional art of wearing kimono is seamlessly and joyfully integrated into the unique lifestyle, climate, and culture of Florida. Florida Kimono strives to maintain the living directory, archive, and community network for Japanese culture in Florida by attempting to build a statewide network that includes both kimono sellers and enthusiasts, rather than a single local club, school, or business.

It exists for Florida-based kimono collectors, sellers, enthusiasts, educators, and cultural organizations who want a central place to connect, learn, preserve, and celebrate kimono culture.

Our mission

Our mission is to connect and celebrate the kimono community, support a vibrant culture and lifestyle, help members discover their style, and curate a trusted network to elevate every wardrobe and keep these traditions alive for the next generation.

Wear kimono with confidence, anywhere and any season in Florida.

Where We're Going

Our Vision

A Florida where kimono is a living tradition

We envision a Florida where kimono is not a costume or a curiosity, but a living, breathing part of everyday life — worn to festivals and tea gatherings, to the beach boardwalk and the botanical garden, in every season this state offers.

A statewide network where every collector knows where to find their next piece, every seller finds an appreciative home for theirs, and every newcomer is met with warmth, guidance, and an open tansu.

How We Wear

Principles of Kitsuke

Kitsuke (着付け) is the art of dressing in kimono. These principles guide how we practice, teach, and share it — with respect for the tradition and room for Florida's own spirit.

左前を避ける

Left over right

The left panel always crosses over the right. It is the first rule every wearer learns, and the foundation everything else is built upon — right over left is reserved only for dressing the deceased.

季節感

Seasonality

Kimono follows the seasons in fabric, lining, motif, and color. In Florida we honor kisetsukan while adapting to our climate — light awase, hitoe, and breathable weaves carry us through long summers.

時・所・場合

Time, place, occasion

TPO — matching formality to the moment. A yukata for the festival, houmongi for the ceremony, komon for the coffee shop. Knowing what to wear, and when, is a kindness to your hosts and yourself.

清潔

Care and cleanliness

Kitsuke begins before the kimono goes on: clean hands, prepared undergarments, pressed collars, and garments stored with care. Respecting the garment extends its life for the next generation of wearers.

調和

Harmony and balance

A clean collar line, a level hem, an obi tied with intention. Kitsuke seeks harmony between garment and wearer — not perfection, but balance, so the kimono moves with you rather than against you.

稽古

Practice and patience

Nobody ties a perfect otaiko on the first try. Kitsuke is keiko — ongoing practice. We celebrate every attempt, share what we learn, and never gatekeep. Wrinkles are proof you showed up.
